# Practice brief: the toolbar

Fictional practice material for the AI-First Design Workshop. Not Excalidraw's roadmap or research.

We're the team that owns Excalidraw. Last week we ran short user tests with six people who had never used it: two product designers, a teacher, a student, an engineering manager and a small business owner. Each one sketched a simple flow chart on a laptop while thinking out loud. This page sums up what they ran into with the toolbar at the top of the screen.

These are problems, not solutions. Fixing them is your job.

## What we saw

1. **Icons without words.** Most people hovered over buttons one by one to learn what they do. The sticky note was read as "comments" or "chat", and the text tool's A as "font settings".
   - "I'm playing a guessing game with these little pictures." (P2)
   - "I thought that one opens a chat." (P5, about the sticky note)

2. **The lock on the far left.** Nobody guessed that it keeps a tool picked after you draw. Two people thought it locked the whole board, and one worried it was about privacy.
   - "Is my drawing locked? Can other people see it?" (P4)

3. **The little letters in the corners.** Four of six didn't notice them. The two who did didn't know they were keyboard shortcuts.
   - "Why does the circle say O? Is that a version thing?" (P1)

4. **Hidden tools.** The three dots at the end hide images, frames and the laser pointer. Four people looked for a way to add an image and gave up, and nobody opened the dots on their own.
   - "I thought the dots were settings, so I didn't touch them." (P6)

5. **Hand or arrow.** Three people picked the hand to grab and move a shape, then wondered why the whole canvas moved instead.
   - "I grabbed it but my box didn't move. Everything moved." (P3)

6. **Back to the arrow after every shape.** After drawing one box, the toolbar switches back to selection. Five people drew a box, then started dragging again and got a selection rectangle.
   - "Wait, where did my rectangle tool go? I have to click it every time?" (P2)

7. **The hint under the toolbar.** The gray line under the toolbar was the only explanation of these behaviors, and nobody read it.

## What success looks like

- A first-time user picks the right tool for a task on the first try, without hovering to find it.
- People understand what the lock does, or never need it.
- Adding an image is easy to find.
- Keyboard shortcuts are there for people who want them, without getting in the way of people who don't.
- It still looks and feels like Excalidraw: hand-drawn, light and quick.
